Keep time for what matters.

How Travedise looks, moves and speaks. Every rule on this page comes back to one of three principles. When a case is not covered, ask which choice keeps all three.

01

Unhurried

Nothing rushes. Wide margins, one idea per section, content that arrives slowly while the interface answers at once. We never create urgency: no countdowns, no "only 2 left".

02

Real

Real places in real light, photos left as they were taken. The colour comes from the photographs; the interface stays a quiet warm neutral so the photos can speak.

03

Specific

Months, hours, prices and place names, never adjectives. Every word, icon and animation has one job, and you can say what it is.

Choosing a colour

What background?
 ├── The page → paper
 ├── A section that must read as separate, with no line → mist
 ├── A panel, tag, icon tile or photo placeholder → wash
 └── The one closing band or CTA card on a page → ink (umber for panels inside it)

What text colour?
 ├── Headings, buttons, links, anything you click → ink
 ├── Paragraphs and descriptions → bark
 ├── Captions, meta, labels, the line under a field → clay   (never smaller than 13px)
 └── On ink → paper, then stone, then dune

A line?
 ├── Form field, select, outline chip → dune (needs 3:1)
 ├── Accordion divider, sticky header edge, selected tab → stone
 └── Anything else → no line. Use space, mist or wash.
Do

Separate sections with space or a mist background. Let a photograph carry the colour of a page.

Don't

Add an accent colour, a gradient, orange or yellow. Don't tint, filter or colour-grade photos to match the UI. Don't use opacity to make a lighter text colour; use the next token.

Tokens: --paper --mist --wash --sand --stone --dune --clay --bark --umber --ink --error --ok in src/styles/global.css. Never a raw hex in a component.

  • One italic phrase per heading, at the end. "Which coast, and when". It is the house signature; two italic phrases cancel it out. Never italic for emphasis in body copy.
  • Headings are weight 300. Never bold a serif heading. Grotesk uses 400 for reading, 500 for labels and buttons, 600 only for small uppercase labels.
  • Nothing under 13px. Body is 18px; lines stop at 64 characters (max-width:64ch).
  • Balance headings, pretty paragraphs. text-wrap:balance on headings, pretty on paragraphs.
  • Numbers that line up use tabular figures. Prices, days and times in lists: font-variant-numeric:tabular-nums.
  • Sentence case everywhere, including buttons and navigation. Uppercase only for 12–13px labels, with .06em tracking.

Photos, chosen by colour

A page feels calm when its photos share a colour family. Every photo the site uses is measured by pnpm photo-colours and written to src/data/photo-colours.json: its family, warmth, lightness, how busy it is, five main colours, and whether white text can sit in the bottom-left.

Rules

  • Photos side by side share a family, or at least a temperature. In a card row, keep warmth on the same side of 0. A blue Maldives card next to two brown temple cards looks like a mistake.
  • One loud photo per view. Only one photo on screen with colourfulness above 0.7. The rest calm it down.
  • Pair busy with calm. Next to a photo with busy above 0.55, use one under 0.4.
  • Text over a photo needs whiteText: true (the bottom-left is dark enough), plus the standard scrim. If it is false, move the text beside the photo instead.
  • Never tint, filter or grade a photo to fit. Fix white balance only. If the colour is wrong for the page, choose another photo.
  • A section takes the colour of its photo, not the reverse. Backgrounds stay paper, mist or wash whatever the photo.
  • People doing things, not posing. Real places, named. No AI-generated places, ever.
  • Pinned families. When the eye disagrees with the numbers, pin the family in scripts/photo-colours.mjs with a reason.

Icons

Two sets, both drawn by us, both in ink. UI icons sit beside words and help you act. Spot icons introduce an idea, the way a chapter number would. The source of every drawing is scripts/icons.mjs; pnpm icons writes the SVG files to src/assets/icons/.

UI icons · 24 grid · 1.5 stroke

  • 24 × 24 grid, drawing inside 20 × 20 (2px padding). Round shapes may touch the 10px circle; square ones stay inside it.
  • 1.5 stroke, round caps and joins, no fills. A dot is a filled circle of 0.9 radius.
  • Points on whole or half pixels so 1x screens stay sharp.
  • Use at 20 or 24px only. At 16px redraw with a 1.25 stroke; never scale a 24 drawing down.
  • stroke="currentColor", so the icon takes the colour of its text.

Spot icons · 72 grid · 1.25 stroke

  • Drawn at 72, shown on a wash tile at least 2.5 times its size. One spot icon per card; never inline with text.
  • Geometric and open. Circles, arcs and straight lines; leave paths open where the eye can close them. One dot of detail at most, dotted lines for routes and rows.
  • No people's faces, no flags, no religious symbols beyond architecture, and nothing that is a cliché of the place (no elephant silhouette for "Sri Lanka").
  • Scale between 56 and 96px only. Below 56 use a UI icon; above 96 redraw with the same 1.25 stroke.

Using icons

  • Icons sit beside words; they do not replace them. The only icon-only buttons are close, menu, play and the arrows in a carousel, and each has an aria-label naming the action ("Close menu", not "Icon").
  • Chevron means "opens here", arrow means "goes there". Accordions and dropdowns use chevron-down; links and buttons that navigate use arrow-right; a link that leaves the site uses arrow-up-right.
  • 8px between an icon and its label (6px at 13–15px text). Optical centre: nudge play 1px right in a circle.
  • One set only. Never mix in Lucide, Phosphor or an emoji, even for one missing icon. Draw it here, following this page, and add it to scripts/icons.mjs.
  • An icon button is 44 × 44 even when the icon is 24.

Motion: respond fast, arrive slowly.

The interface answers you at once: a press, a hover, a menu open in a fifth of a second. Content takes its time: headings rise, photos settle, sections unfold as you reach them, the way a place comes into view. Scrolling is smoothed with Lenis; scroll reveals run on motion.dev. Nothing bounces, nothing loops, nothing moves on its own.

The five signature moves

Your system asks for reduced motion, so the demos jump to their end state. That is exactly what the site does for you.

1 · Rise

From the teato the sea.

The hero heading, set in lines (.ln). Each line rises from behind a mask: y 105% → 0, 900ms ease-out, 80ms per line. Once per page per visit.

2 · Unfold

Cards, rows, paragraphs. opacity 0 → 1, y 24px → 0, 900ms ease-out, 70ms apart, at most 6 staggered.

3 · Settle

Photos. Fade in over 600ms while the image scales 1.08 → 1 over 1400ms, ease-out. The wash behind it means there is never a blank box.

Feedback · press and open
Can we change a tour?

Yes. Every tour is a starting point: move a night, add a stop, slow it down.

Is the price per person?

Yes, sharing a room. We quote single rooms on request.

Press: scale(.97) in 160ms. Accordions animate their height in 320ms ease-in-out; the plus turns 45°.

Smooth scrolling (Lenis)

  • Wheel only. lerp: 0.1, smoothWheel: true. Touch scrolling stays native (syncTouch: false); phones already scroll well.
  • Off for reduced motion, and paused while a menu or sheet is open (lenis.stop()).
  • Anchors and "jump to" links go through Lenis with the header height as the offset.
  • Inner scroll areas keep native scrolling: add data-lenis-prevent to menus, carousels and any element with its own scrollbar.

Scroll reveals (motion.dev)

  • Once. A reveal plays the first time an element is 15% in view, and never again on that page.
  • Nothing above the fold is hidden. Elements already in view when the page loads show at once; only the hero heading rises, and only once per visit.
  • The page reads without JavaScript. Hidden start states apply only when the script has run and motion is allowed (html.m-on).
  • One container, one entrance. A grid unfolds as one group; never reveal each paragraph of an article.
  • Never on what people read to decide. Prices, filter results, form fields, the month planner: these change instantly.
  • Only transform and opacity (and height for accordions). No blur, no clip-path on large photos.

Reduced motion

When the system asks for reduced motion: no smooth scroll, no reveals, no drift, no rise. Everything is in place when the page loads. Menus and accordions open instantly. Colour changes on hover stay, because they are not movement.

Writing

We write as the team: travel designers in Galle and Bundeena who have been to every place we name. Plain, calm, specific, honest about the downsides. The full voice, with examples from the travel notes, is in .claude/skills/travedise-voice/.

The six rules

  • Short sentences, one idea each. No exclamation marks.
  • Say the hard part. "Leopards are never promised, only made likely."
  • Facts over adjectives. Months, hours, prices and drive times. Never invent one: mark it [CHECK: …].
  • Advice, not description. Every paragraph helps someone decide.
  • No contractions, no em dashes, British spelling, sentence case.
  • One italic phrase per heading, at the end.
